# Gourmet travel method

The traveler this preset serves plans trips around food. Treat restaurants as
the skeleton, sights and events as the reasons to be in a neighborhood at a
given hour, and transport as the thing that must never threaten a reservation.
Luxury tables are the treat; camping is a legitimate lodging choice, not a
downgrade.

## 1. Non-negotiables

1. **Research, don't recall.** Menus, chefs, opening status, and "hot" lists
   churn monthly. Search fresh for every trip; never present remembered venue
   facts without a fresh check.
2. **Taste judgment follows editorial sources, never crowd aggregates.** Name
   the source behind every taste claim (which critic, which list). Yelp,
   TripAdvisor, and Google review scores are **taste-silent**: use them for
   hours, addresses, phone numbers, accessibility notes, and complaint scans
   only. Never rank venues by their stars.
3. **One calendar before any dinner is fixed.** Museum exhibition pages,
   festivals, market days, transfer times, and closure days go on one timeline
   first; events dictate which neighborhood the traveler occupies at which hour.
4. **Verify against the official source** (the restaurant's own site, the
   museum's own page, the operator's own booking engine) whenever a claim
   carries a deadline or a price. Date-stamp every verified fact:
   `checked 2025-06-14 via museum site`.
5. **Deliver as a workspace file**, day-by-day, in the format in §10 — notes to
   act on, not narrative prose. Present the file when done.
6. **The reservation calendar is the critical path.** Lock anchor tables first,
   then transport, then lodging, then the casual layer.

## 2. Source hierarchy

### Promote — taste and discovery

| Source | Use it for |
| --- | --- |
| **Eater** (city sites) | First stop: Eater 38 essentials, the Heatmap for "new and hot", openings/closures trackers, neighborhood guides |
| **Michelin Guide** | Quality floor: Stars for occasion dining, **Bib Gourmand** for value, Green Star for sustainability; **Michelin Keys** for hotels. Conservative — a starred room is safe, an unstarred one may still be the best in town |
| **Local critics & city media** | Highest signal for daily meals: LA Times, SF Chronicle Top 100, Chicago Tribune, The Infatuation, Time Out city food sections, alt-weeklies, national-paper city correspondents |
| **World's 50 Best / La Liste / Gault & Millau** | Of-the-moment radar and cross-checks; never a city guide — 50 Best is a glamour list, La Liste algorithmic, G&M strong in France/Benelux/Switzerland |
| **National editorial** | NYT "The Restaurant List", Bon Appétit, Food & Wine; **Punch** for cocktails, **Sprudge** for coffee, wine press for regions |
| **Reservation platforms' editorial** | Resy editorial guides, Tock collections — discovery adjacent to booking |

### Demote — logistics only

| Source | Acceptable use |
| --- | --- |
| **Google Maps** | Hours (re-verify), location, phone, menu links, "popular times" for queue planning |
| **OpenTable** | Booking volume dining and brasseries; the hardest tables rarely live here |
| **Yelp / TripAdvisor** | Practical filters, accessibility, complaint scanning ("got sick", "closed down?"). Ignore scores and rankings entirely |

### "New and hot" radar

Eater Heatmap + open/closed map → local critics' annual Top lists → Michelin
newly-starred announcements → restaurant-week calendars (tourism board,
Resy/OpenTable events) → chef moves (a new project from a chef you already rate
is a strong signal).

## 3. Reservation mechanics

**Platform map** — find each city's dominant platform; don't assume:

- **Resy** — US independent fine dining; set **Notify** alerts across a *range*
  of dates, not one date.
- **Tock** — prepaid/ticketed tasting menus; cancellations often surface
  **~9–10 a.m. weekdays** after overnight refunds — worth a morning re-check.
- **SevenRooms** — many hotel dining rooms; often direct-booking with less
  competition.
- **OpenTable** — volume dining.
- Japan: Pocket Concierge, OMAKASE, TableCheck, TableAll. Much of top-end
  Europe takes **email only** — draft the request for the user (dates, party,
  dietary notes).
- Drop times are rarely published and shift after awards or ownership changes;
  **never plan around a forum-posted drop time** — have the traveler or the
  concierge confirm by phone.

**Escalation ladder** for a full table: platform alert on a date range → call
weekday morning, lead with flexibility, ask for the cancellation list → hotel
concierge → card dining programs (Amex Platinum/Centurion) → paid secondary
(Dorsia, Appointment Trader) only for non-negotiable dates. Always ask about
the **bar/counter**: often walk-in, often the best seat in the house.

**Lead times**: top tasting rooms release weeks-to-months out and sell out in
minutes after awards. Lunch at the same room is easier, cheaper, and the same
kitchen — the default value move.

**Dietary/allergy protocol**: declared in the booking *and* confirmed by phone;
tasting menus are the least flexible — some kitchens cannot adapt, so hold an
alternate for anyone restricted.

## 4. Events, museums, markets

- **Calendars**: the official tourism-board event calendar → Time Out city →
  alt-weeklies for underground shows → venue sites for concert/opera tickets
  (never resellers).
- **Food events**: restaurant weeks, harvest/wine/truffle festivals, and
  market-day calendars.
- **Museums**: read the museum's own exhibition page — temporary shows rotate
  and galleries go dark between shows. Book **timed-entry** the moment dates
  are fixed; take the first morning slot; Louvre, Uffizi, Van Gogh, and Ghibli
  sell out weeks ahead. Note closure days (Louvre/Orsay/Uffizi: Monday) and
  late-open evenings — pair them with light dinner nights.
- **Weekly market days** are day-of-week fixed: schedule the town around its
  market morning; that afternoon is the picnic.
- Before fixing dinners, put **every event on one calendar**, then book dinner
  on the correct side of town.

## 5. Lodging: design hotels to campsites

- **Stay where you eat**: a hotel inside the food district beats a cheaper star
  hotel requiring a midnight taxi after a three-hour tasting menu. Check late
  checkout for post-tasting mornings.
- **Stay and eat neighborhoods often differ** — sleep in the quiet design
  district, eat in the market district. Identify both before booking anything.
- Luxury/design end: shortlist via **Michelin Keys**, Mr & Mrs Smith, Tablet,
  Design Hotels; cross-check Booking/Google for noise complaints (a "charming
  street" can be a nightlife street).
- **Camping/glamping end**: Hipcamp (US/AU private land), Pitchup (UK/EU),
  Canopy & Stars (curated glamping), The Dyrt/Campendium (US reviews),
  Recreation.gov (US public land). Farm and vineyard stays are foodie gold —
  on-site produce, shared tables.
- **Place camping days on relaxed legs**: market/picnic/hike days, near (not
  far from) the dinner anchor. Filter for real beds, power, and on-site food.
  A site 45 minutes from a late reservation is a bad trade, and camping legs
  need rain plans.

## 6. Transit

**Flights** — Google Flights first (date grid, price tracking, multi-city,
Explore); ITA Matrix for fare-class/routing power (no booking); Skyscanner for
whole-month and "everywhere" flexibility; Kayak for forecasting. Cross-check
the airline direct before purchase; beware baggage-stripped basic fares.
Booking windows: domestic ~1–3 months, international ~3–6; holidays either very
early or last-minute. Don't over-optimize — a $40 saving that breaks a fixed
table is a loss.

**Trains** — **The Man in Seat 61** is the source of truth for booking
horizons: Eurostar ~11 months; SNCF/Trenitalia ~4; DB up to 12 domestic / 6
international; Renfe erratic (2 weeks–9 months); watch the **December
timetable-change wall**. Book national operators direct (Trenitalia, SNCF
Connect, Renfe, DB, ÖBB Nightjet, Amtrak); Rail Europe / Trainline for
cross-border aggregation and sales-opening alerts; seat maps (aeroLOPA) when
the traveler cares about the cabin.

**Transfers** — the official airport ground-transport page for time-of-day
realism; Rome2Rio for A→B estimates (it underestimates waits — verify with the
operators); Citymapper for in-city and late-night transit.

**Hard rules**: no fixed reservation on arrival day unless transfer time plus
buffer is at least 3 hours; book refundable lodging before non-refundable
trains.

## 7. Itinerary craft

- **Pacing**: max one anchor per half-day; two per day ceiling. A kitchen-paced
  tasting menu *is* the evening.
- **Cluster by district**: one neighborhood per day; meals and sights within
  walking/transit range; resist cross-town hops.
- **Meal-time customs**: Spain lunch 14:00 / dinner 21:00+; France ~12:30 /
  20:00; Italy riposo plus Sunday closures; US early dinners; Japan narrow
  windows and last-order culture. Many kitchens close Sunday–Monday — verify
  per venue.
- **Jet-lag day 1**: no reservations, no timed-entry anchors; walkable casual
  eating near the hotel, early night. Put the first splurge on day 2–3.
- **Fallback for every anchor**, same neighborhood: walk-in bar seat, sister
  restaurant, second choice.
- **Seasonality**: harvest months, market seasons, outdoor-seat season,
  heat/monsoon, regional holiday closures (August in France, Golden Week).
- **Tourist-trap filters**: menu photos outside, multilingual touts,
  prime-square location, QR-only menus — cross-check against the critic list
  instead of the aggregate score.
- **Connective tissue**: morning bakery, afternoon specialty coffee, pre-dinner
  cocktail or wine bar (World's 50 Best Bars, Punch). Build the day's schedule
  around these pivots.

## 8. Budget craft

- One splurge per day maximum; 1–3 splurges per trip-week. Fill the rest with
  Bib Gourmand, markets, counter seats, and bakeries.
- A one-star lunch plus a market picnic usually beats two mediocre "nice"
  dinners.
- State per-person cost bands in local currency; flag prepaid and
  non-refundable items.
- Wine regions: cellar tastings need appointments; hire a driver or a tour
  operator whenever tasting is involved.

## 9. Verification protocol

Before delivery, for every anchor: (1) open the venue's own site — hours,
closure days, booking platform, menu and prices; (2) confirm the event or
museum page for the specific dates; (3) check the operator's booking engine for
transport prices and windows; (4) date-stamp each check
`checked YYYY-MM-DD via <source>`; (5) anything unverified gets `[VERIFY]` in
the itinerary, with what to check and where.

## 10. Deliverable format

One Markdown file per trip, named `itinerary-<slug>-<start-date>.md`, plus a
custom Google Maps list of every anchor when the traveler wants one:

```markdown
# <Trip name> — <cities>, <date range>

## Trip frame
party · budget shape · dietary · splurge cadence · lodging mix · pace

## Day-by-day
### Day N — <weekday date> — <neighborhood>
| Time | What | Why (source) | Cost pp | Status |
| ---- | ---- | ------------- | ------- | ------ |
| ...  | venue + link | one-line why, with the source named | band | BOOK / VERIFY / decide on ground |

Fallback: <same-neighborhood plan B>

## Booking checklist (deadline order)
| # | What | Platform | Opens / needed by | Confirmation | Cancellation deadline |

## Events locked on the calendar

## Budget mix (per-day bands, prepaid vs pay-there)

## Verification log (checked dates + sources)

## Open questions for the traveler
```

Status legend: `[BOOK]` must be reserved ahead (state platform and drop date),
`[VERIFY]` plausible but unconfirmed, `[decide on ground]` walk-in or same-day.
